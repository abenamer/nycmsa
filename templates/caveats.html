Caveats

  • These are estimates, not official figures. The model is a simplified version of the DOE's matching process: each program has a cutoff for your priority group, and your child clears it or not. Nobody outside the DOE knows this year's cutoffs.
  • Everything rests on last year's numbers. Seats, applicants, and offers per priority group are last year's figures as reported on MySchools. This year's seats, applicant pool, and rules can differ. Published offers include waitlist offers, so first-round offers are estimated.
  • The figures are read automatically. MySchools states them as rounded prose ("about 230 applicants in this group and about 140 received offers"), which this site parses by machine. Nine programs were entered by hand from screenshots. A misreading is possible; the numbers page shows what the model uses, so you can check it against MySchools.
  • Test scores describe the school, not your chances. They are the share of the school's students at proficiency on the state ELA and math tests, read automatically from the DOE's published spreadsheets for the year shown. The reader is tested against sample files and cross-checked after each download, but a change in the DOE's layout could still misread a value.
  • Screened programs are approximated. Your answer about test placement stands in for any screen, including grade-based ones; the DOE's academic groups are not modelled.
  • Waitlists are an assumption. The DOE says each waitlist draws a new number; in 2022 some schools kept the old one. Try both settings.
